01 / About

I became curious about people before I became curious about institutions.

Icome from the Levant, a region where identities, communities and borders have always overlapped. Diversity was not something I first met in a classroom. It was around me.

My early work was built around dialogue, young people and participation. I wanted to meet people with different backgrounds, listen to how they saw their societies, and create spaces where disagreement did not have to mean distance.

Over time, another question kept returning: who shapes the policies that affect these societies, and why do institutions act the way they do? That question slowly moved me from practice towards research.

02 / Research

What I am trying to understand now.

Question 01

How does the EU act when a neighbouring society goes through political change?

This question grew from my research on the EU response to Syria after December 2024.

Question 02

When institutions and Member States share responsibility, what makes their external action coherent?

I am interested in the legal, institutional and political organisation behind what we often simply call “EU action”.

Question 03

Do the same patterns appear outside the European neighbourhood?

That curiosity has taken me towards EU-Myanmar relations and the possibility of comparison across political transitions.

Current direction

I am not trying to lock myself into one country or one region. The Euro-Mediterranean space is where much of my experience comes from, but comparison helps me see whether the patterns I notice are local — or part of something wider.

04 / Selected work

Different roles, one long conversation about participation and policy.

Tawleh

Creating space for dialogue

I founded Tawleh as a civic dialogue platform. It grew from a simple belief that people need spaces to disagree, participate and remain curious about one another.

Y-PEER / youth networks

Learning through people

I coordinated a national youth network with hundreds of members, worked with peer educators and spent years facilitating conversations on participation, equality and civic life.

UNDP / policy practice

From participation to policy

At UNDP Syria, I worked on civil society policy and regulatory reform, including policy papers, workshops and stakeholder engagement.

MARA-Méditerranée

Back to the Mediterranean

My current professional work brings together advocacy, partnerships and regional cooperation across the Mediterranean — now seen through the eyes of someone increasingly rooted in research.

06 / Elsewhere

The world makes more sense to me when I can move through it.

I like cities, languages, photography and the small differences in how people organise everyday life. Travelling is not separate from how I think about policy. It reminds me that institutions eventually meet real streets, families, habits and memories.

I am usually happiest walking through a place I do not know yet, carrying a camera, noticing details, and trying to understand how people make a place their own.
