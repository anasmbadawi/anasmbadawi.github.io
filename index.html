01 / About

I am interested in how the world around us gets governed.

My work grew from practice. For around a decade, I have worked with young people, civil society, dialogue initiatives and international organisations across the Mediterranean and Europe.

I come from the Levant, a region where different identities, communities and political realities meet every day. That made me curious about people before it made me curious about institutions. I wanted to understand how people with very different backgrounds live together, disagree, organise and still build common spaces.

My early work was centred on youth participation, dialogue and civil society. Over time, that experience led me to a different question: who shapes the policies that affect these societies, and how?

I moved from taking part in policy spaces to trying to understand how those policies are made.

That is where my work is today: somewhere between policy practice and academic research, with the Euro-Mediterranean region as an important point of reference — but not a boundary.

02 / Research

From practice to research.

My academic work asks how European external action is organised, how different institutions and Member States interact, and whether their actions remain coherent during periods of political change.

I studied international relations and public diplomacy at Jagiellonian University and the University of Siena, and later European affairs at the College of Europe in Natolin. These studies gave me a legal and institutional language for questions I had already met in practice.

Current exploration

EU external action during political transitions

I am developing a comparative research direction on how legal, institutional and political organisation shapes coherence in EU and Member State action during political transitions.

Syria

The EU response after the fall of the Assad regime

My College of Europe thesis examined how the division of competences affected the coherence of EU reactions and actions towards Syria after December 2024. It received the Prize for an Outstanding Master Thesis in European Neighbourhood Studies.

Myanmar

Looking beyond my own region

I am now exploring EU–Myanmar relations after 2011 as a comparison. The point is not only to study another case, but to ask whether patterns I see in the European neighbourhood tell us something broader about EU external action.

Methods

Tracing decisions, institutions and policy choices

My work draws mainly on process tracing, legal analysis, qualitative document analysis, discourse analysis and structured comparison.

04 / Elsewhere

Beyond the desk.

Movement has been a recurring part of my life.

I have studied, worked and travelled across different parts of Europe and the Mediterranean. I rarely experienced these places only through institutions; I like to understand them through streets, conversations, languages, food, photography and the small differences in everyday life.

Travel is not separate from how I think. It is one of the ways I remind myself that policy documents describe real places, and that every system looks different when seen from inside it.
